# Sequential Art Free Tutorials: Practical Online Guides, Video Essays and Exercises on Layout, Lettering and Visual Storytelling

*Research date: 2026-11-17. Scope: free online tutorials, video essays, blog guides and exercises that teach the technical craft of comics with worked examples, sorted into three lists: **panel transitions, gutter pacing and page layout grids** (A), **speech balloon placement, lettering rules and sound effect design** (B) and **visual storytelling techniques such as emanata, framing and mood lighting** (C). Every bullet links the resource itself and says what that link teaches. Each list runs from the first thing to study to the most advanced. The last two sections list the leads that were dropped and what is inferred. This note goes one level deeper than [sequential-art-learning-resources.md](sequential-art-learning-resources.md): it links single articles, episodes and lessons, and it leaves out books and paid courses.*

**How the sources were verified**

- "Free" means the page could be read or the video watched in full without paying and without an account. Where that is in doubt (one course asks for a free enrolment), the bullet says so.
- "Practical" means the page shows a worked example on a real page or panel, a step-by-step process, or an exercise the reader can do.
- Every bullet was checked on the resource's own page: the article, the creator's site, the school's page or the official channel. Search results and listicles were used only to find leads.
- Pages were read through a fetch tool that returns an extraction of the page. Sites that return nothing to that tool were read in a browser pane: comicsdevices.com, dw-wp.com, sevencamels.blogspot.com, kleinletters.com, and the Blambot infographics, which are images.
- No bot check or CAPTCHA was solved. In the browser pane, comicsdevices.com and one Tumblr blog first showed an automatic connection check that cleared without any input; no PDF was opened and nothing was downloaded.
- YouTube titles, channel names, lengths and descriptions were read from each video's own watch page. A video summary rests on the title and the description; no video was watched. Where the description says nothing about the content, the bullet says the summary rests on the title alone.
- A bullet marked *(also in sequential-art-learning-resources.md)* is carried over from that file. Nine carried-over items were not opened again in this pass and rest on that file's verification of the same day: the Center for Cartoon Studies day 4, Steve Ellis's article, Kibuishi's tutorial, Shooter's memo, Blambot's grammar page, the Comicraft glossary, the car-chase tutorial, Manga Morphology and Matthew Wilson's lesson. The other carried-over items were opened again and are now broken down into their parts.
- A creator's standing is given as the resource or the creator's own site states it. The last section lists the cases where it could not be confirmed first-hand.

## A. Panel Transitions, Gutter Pacing and Page Layout Grids

- **[The Basic Blocks: Arrangement and Layout](https://comicsdevices.com/the-basics/three/)** — Reimena Yee (cartoonist; curator of the Creator's Guide to Comics Devices). Article in a free online reference library. Teaches what a grid does to time (a regular beat) and what happens when one panel leaves it, by redrawing a six-panel party scene so that the last panel detaches from the grid as the character leaves the conversation. It then shows a dominant panel, a full bleed and a gutter that carries the weight of a moment on published pages.
  - [Panels and Gutters](https://comicsdevices.com/the-basics/one/): defines the panel as one storytelling instance and the gutter as the place where the reader infers what happened (closure).
  - [Contraction](https://comicsdevices.com/contraction/): shrinking panels or narrowing gutters over a sequence to speed the pace or raise tension, shown on several pages; it adds the rule that the panel with the smallest gap to the last one is read next. The [list of all devices](https://comicsdevices.com/whole/) has the matching entries Expansion, Flow, Tracking, Page/Screen Flip, Match-cutting and Cross-cutting.
  - [Understanding Your Craft with Comics Devices with Reimena Yee](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PLYL6f8nGEw): a recorded talk (1:46:43, Cartoonists United channel) in which she walks through the devices; the description calls it a more advanced look at how comics work.
- **[One-Week Cartooning Workout, Day 4: Laying It Out](https://sites.google.com/cartoonstudies.org/owcw/day-4)** — The Center for Cartoon Studies (cartooning school). Exercise in a free self-paced course of eight days. Teaches pacing by editing: break an anecdote into at most 24 thumbnails, one index card per panel, then rearrange, cut and add cards until the story flows. *(also in sequential-art-learning-resources.md)*
- **[Five Card Nancy](https://scottmccloud.com/4-inventions/nancy/index.html)** — Scott McCloud (cartoonist, author of *Understanding Comics*). Exercise in the form of a card game for three or more players. Teaches panel-to-panel juxtaposition by play: each player holds five single panels cut from *Nancy* strips and adds one to a growing strip, and the others judge whether the new panel works after the last one. The page gives the rules, the way to make a deck and three variants.
- **[Activity: panel lottery, an exercise in narrative juxtaposition and editing](https://dw-wp.com/2010/05/panel-lottery-an-exercise-in-narrative-juxtaposition-and-editing/)** — Jessica Abel and Matt Madden (cartoonists, authors of *Drawing Words & Writing Pictures*). Group exercise. Teaches McCloud's panel transitions without a stock of *Nancy* panels: everyone draws unrelated panels on index cards from a list of prompts, the cards go into a box, and the group then builds a sequence one card at a time and argues whether each card follows.
- **[Grids & Gestures](https://spinweaveandcut.com/grids-gestures/)** — Nick Sousanis (Eisner-winning comics artist, professor at San Francisco State University). Exercise of 5 to 10 minutes. Teaches the page as a map of time: divide one sheet into a grid that has the shape of your day, then fill each cell with marks that carry its activity or mood, with no recognisable drawings. The page adds a week-long diary version and a version that builds a page from the composition of a building.
- **[Pro Artist's Guide to Comic & Manga Layouts, Paneling, Flow](https://clipstudio.net/how-to-draw/archives/160963)** — Steve Ellis (comics artist; Art Rocket, Celsys). Article. Teaches gutter width as a pacing control (uniform, wide, borderless, overlapping), the uses of insets, splash panels and broken panels, and six planning questions for a page, the last of them how the final panel leads to the next page. *(also in sequential-art-learning-resources.md)*
- **[Quickguides: basic layout](https://dw-wp.com/2011/11/quickguides-layout/)** — Jessica Abel and Matt Madden. Step-by-step article with seven photos, the first of a series on ruling a page. Teaches how to rule a live area of 12 by 16 inches with true right angles on bristol board, with a T-square and tick marks, in seven steps. *(also in sequential-art-learning-resources.md)*
  - [Quickguides: more layout tips](https://dw-wp.com/2011/11/quickguides-layout-tips/): getting the right page ratio without measuring, by tracing a printed comic in one corner and extending its diagonal; and tick marks on a master sheet to rule a stack of pages alike, gutters included.
  - [Quickguides: bleeds](https://dw-wp.com/2011/11/quickguides-bleeds/): the three boxes of a bleed page (live area, trim, bleed), with a photo of a page that was laid out without them.
  - [Video: laying out a page](https://dw-wp.com/2010/07/video-laying-out-a-page/): four short videos from a workshop demonstration by one of the two authors, on live area, original size and ratio, ruling the live area, and ruling the tiers.
  - [Guest Post: Paulo Patricio on how to make a panel grid in five minutes](https://dw-wp.com/2014/03/5min-grid/): Paulo Patrício (illustrator and cartoonist) cuts a six-panel grid out of cardboard and traces it onto every page; merging cells gives the variants. New in this file.
- **[Controlling Time and Attention through Comic Panels](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UHNTtkV3Ed8)** — Tom Hart (cartoonist, director of the Sequential Artists Workshop). Video lesson, 24:28, taken from his storytelling course. Teaches how panel choice controls time and the reader's attention; the description gives no more than that, so the summary rests on the title.
  - [SAW Curriculum: Page Compositions Part 1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5rDVFgafM_8) (3:45): a quick look at designing a page from the action inside it and the panels chosen to show it.
  - [Storytelling with The Odyssey of Sergeant Jack Brennan](https://sequentialartistsworkshop.thinkific.com/courses/comicsodyssey), the free course these belong to: its units "How Pictures Work in Time" (Implying Time, Juxtaposition of Images) and "How Pictures Work on the Page" (The Size and Shape of the Panels, Composing the Page) cover this list. The page offers the course through a "Get Started for Free" enrolment, so it probably needs an account. *(also in sequential-art-learning-resources.md)*
- **[Mastering Panel Composition](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sSecXc1Xy7E)** — Aaron Conley (comics artist, the page-and-panel instructor of Marvel's course on Proko). Video lesson, 9:01, on Proko's channel. Teaches how to vary the panels of a page: quick sketches of full-figure shots and full backgrounds to set tone and context, close-ups and action shots for energy, and the nine-panel grid as a base layout.
- **[How To Layout a Comic Book Page | Terminal Part 1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eYJSq-ybdP4)** — David Finch (comics artist for Image, Marvel and DC). Video tutorial, 26:32, on his own channel. Teaches a professional's order of work on one page of his book *Terminal*: reading the script, layouts, thumbnails and perspective grids.
- **[Copper: Step-by-Step](https://boltcity.com/workshop/copper_tutorial)** — Kazu Kibuishi (cartoonist, author of the strip *Copper*). Step-by-step article. Teaches one page from start to finish: a thumbnail revised for pacing, then every panel laid out before any pencilling. [Part 2](https://boltcity.com/workshop/copper_tutorial/tutorial_02.htm) covers ruling the panel borders and hand-lettering. *(also in sequential-art-learning-resources.md)*
- **[Webtoon 101 [By an Original WEBTOON creator]](https://tips.clip-studio.com/en-us/articles/4143)** — Michi (creator of the WEBTOON Original *Rebirth*, by the author's own statement). Article on Clip Studio Tips. Teaches pacing in a vertical scroll: the gap between panels is the pause, so a wider gap before a reveal holds the reader longer; an action reads best in three panels (anticipation, action, outcome), and every panel added between them slows it.
  - [The Infinite Page of Korean Webcomics | Strip Panel Naked](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6VEhV_ar-EU) (6:19): the same subject as a video essay on *Wind Breaker* and *Sweet Home*.
  - [Laying Out Digital Comics | Jaeger | Strip Panel Naked](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zIMj8W9X2gc) (6:28): how a comic made for phone screens changes the layout choices.
- **[Strip Panel Naked: the grid episodes](https://www.youtube.com/@StripPanelNaked/videos)** — Hassan Otsmane-Elhaou (letterer, editor of *PanelxPanel*). Video essays of 6 to 11 minutes, each on published pages. Together they teach what a fixed grid does and what breaking it means.
  - [The 9-Panel Grid | The Omega Men (2016)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KQzXcFmf98I) (7:25): how the nine-panel grid works and how adjusting it adds subtext.
  - [Passing Time in the 9-Panel Grid | Batman](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=R4IL3CUcObY) (6:14): using the grid to slow time on purpose, on *Batman* #21.
  - [The Middle Panel - 9 Panel Grids | Vision](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gx1DZg7ACoc) (7:53): the weight of the centre panel.
  - [From Hell's 9 Panel Grid](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=o12GA4pGMUo) (9:32). *(also in sequential-art-learning-resources.md)*
  - [The Eight Panel Grid with Stray Bullets](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AVEEocOw1Ek) (8:12): what the eight-panel grid is for and how Lapham uses it.
  - [16 Panel Grids in The Dark Knight Returns](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4_TlTR-k0SM) (11:18): two pages read closely, the opening page and a flashback.
- **[Strip Panel Naked: time and pacing](https://www.youtube.com/@StripPanelNaked/videos)** — Hassan Otsmane-Elhaou. Video essays. Together they teach how panel size, panel count and repetition set the speed of reading.
  - [How Panel Sizes Convey Time in Comics](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z5gRt7X6zvc) (7:26): panel sizes on a structured grid as a timing score, on *Wonder Woman*; its description points on to Nick Sousanis's work.
  - [How to Create More Time in Comic Books](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SejtZb1Iw0U) (6:44): insets and colour changes that reframe inside a panel and so add beats, on *Desolation Jones*.
  - [Staccato Panels in Comics | Jim Steranko & Captain America](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vh6U0K769pA) (6:03): one action split over several small panels to build rhythm.
  - [Slowing Down the Pace in Comics | Felix & Macabber](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fuiCFbXAEd8) (6:50): the host applies the series' lessons to a fight scene in a comic he wrote.
  - [How to Manage Focus and Time in Comic Books](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zs8rDHiIBqQ). *(also in sequential-art-learning-resources.md)*
- **[Strip Panel Naked: transitions, page turns and empty space](https://www.youtube.com/@StripPanelNaked/videos)** — Hassan Otsmane-Elhaou. Video essays. Together they teach how to move between scenes and pages.
  - [Mastering Scene Transitions in Comic Books | The Suicide Forest](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VAzAf3EiXkI) (6:36): ways to juxtapose two scenes or to ease from one into the next, beyond ending a scene at the foot of a page.
  - [Darwyn Cooke & The Punctuation of Comics](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hQj9Cpp293Q) (9:51): the panel edge, the end of a tier and the end of a page as comma, dash and full stop, on *The Score*.
  - [The Soft-Reset Page Turn in Comics | Mockingbird](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FqJvukIB14E) (7:14): treating each page turn as a reset of the narrative style.
  - [Line of Sight in Comics | Petals](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wXoBFGxKVd0) (9:16): how the placement of images on the page shapes how they are read, on a silent comic. *(also in sequential-art-learning-resources.md)*
  - [Empty Space on a Comic Book Page | Animal Man | Grant Morrison](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2dQog1qeUic) (8:22): when white space stops being a gutter. *(also in sequential-art-learning-resources.md)*
  - [Space and Time in Comics | Hedra](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=knKL-DIqwmg) (11:40): spatial relationships across a whole page.
- **[Chapter Five and a Half](http://www.scottmccloud.com/makingcomics/five_half/00.html)** — Scott McCloud. Online chapter in comics form, nine pages and seven pages of notes, an extension of chapter 5 of *Making Comics*. Teaches panel layouts made for a screen and not for a printed page, together with colour art. Its title page says it is not a full guide to webcomics.
- **[Layout Workbook](https://www.tcj.com/layout-workbook/)** — Frank Santoro (cartoonist, columnist of The Comics Journal). Series of columns from 2011, with diagrams; the most advanced item of this list. The first column teaches the proportions behind the North American page (a live area of 6 by 9 inches, two facing pages as a grid of 9 by 12) and sets two exercises: rule a 9 by 12 grid of 1.5-inch squares, then find its centres with a compass.
  - [Layout Workbook 2](https://www.tcj.com/layout-workbook-2/): "giving up the centre". A fixed six-panel grid splits into two columns after about eight pages; a wide centre panel, as Kirby used, pulls the eye back and gives a longer beat. Exercise with graph paper and compass on a Romita Spider-Man spread.
  - [Layout Workbook 3](https://www.tcj.com/layout-workbook-3/): how margins set timing. Without a centre margin a spread reads across both pages like a Sunday strip; shown on his own *Silver Surfer* and *Cold Heat* spreads.
  - [Layout Workbook 5](https://www.tcj.com/layout-workbook-5/): fold a sheet into a spread and construct its centre circle and centre square; the rule of thirds on the 6 by 9 page; tiers in Barks, Kirby and Hergé.
  - [Layout Workbook 10](https://www.tcj.com/layout-workbook-10/): the same compass method used to diagram spreads of Mazzucchelli's *Asterios Polyp*. Parts 7, 8 and 9 exist and were not read.

## B. Speech Balloon Placement, Lettering Rules and Sound Effect Design

- **[Comic Book Grammar & Tradition](https://blambot.com/pages/comic-book-grammar-tradition)** — Nate Piekos (letterer, founder of Blambot). Reference article. Teaches the conventions of lettering as an alphabetical list of some three dozen entries: balloon tails, joined and butted balloons, burst, whisper, thought, radio and telepathic balloons, the crossbar "I", breath marks and sound-effect punctuation. *(also in sequential-art-learning-resources.md)*
- **[The Comicraft Glossary of Lettering Terms](https://balloontales.com/the-comicraft-glossary-of-lettering-terms/)** — John Roshell (letterer and font designer, Comicraft). Reference article. Teaches the working vocabulary of a lettering studio in about sixty terms, among them butting, join, tangent, widow, dead space and open sound effects. *(also in sequential-art-learning-resources.md)*
- **[Balloon Placement](https://kleinletters.com/BalloonPlacement.html)** — Todd Klein (comics letterer and logo designer, by his own site). Article with diagrams. Teaches placement as reading order: number every script item and mark its place on the art before lettering; keep balloons above and away from the speaker and off faces, hands and feet; let the character on the left speak first; lead the eye through a hard layout with a trail of balloons. It shows the same two pages placed well and placed confusingly.
  - [Hand-Lettering Basics](https://kleinletters.com/HandBasics.html): his sheet of tools and techniques, with the technical pens and dip nibs he uses for regular, bold and display letters, and the ink.
- **[Lettering and Balloon Placement Memo](https://jimshooter.com/2011/05/lettering-and-balloon-placement-memo.html/)** — Jim Shooter (comics writer and editor). Article on his own blog. Teaches placement as house rules: balloons off faces and anchored to panel borders, short straight pointers aimed at the mouth, and consecutive balloons of one speaker joined so the order is never in doubt. *(also in sequential-art-learning-resources.md)*
- **[Making Word Balloons](https://balloontales.com/making-word-balloons/)** — John Roshell (Comicraft). Step-by-step article with images, the first of the Balloon Tales tips for Adobe Illustrator. Teaches the base routine: type the text where the balloon will go, draw the ellipse, pull it to a squarer shape, draw the tail with three pen clicks toward the mouth, unite tail and balloon. The principles carry over to any tool.
  - [Word Arrangement](https://balloontales.com/word-arrangement/): stacking a line of dialogue into a rounded block with the widest line in the centre, breaking lines at natural pauses, how tight a balloon may be (a small "o" must fit between text and edge), and narrowing one over-long line to 95 or 90 percent of its width and no further.
  - [Creating Tails and Joins](https://balloontales.com/creating-tails-and-joins/): tails on one invisible arc from the balloon centre to the mouth, joins between two balloons, and a "common mistakes" panel fixed in three passes (a balloon on a head, a balloon in the line of sight, a tail that pokes a mouth).
  - [Butting Balloons](https://balloontales.com/butting-balloons/): cutting a balloon flush to the panel border in five steps, and why to use it only in a tight panel.
  - [Floating Text & Captions](https://balloontales.com/floating-text-captions/): captions set as loose text on the art to save space and set a rhythm; two crowded pages (Spider-Man, X-Men) shown before and after.
  - [Seven Uses for Thought Balloons](https://balloontales.com/seven-uses-for-thought-balloons/): seven jobs a thought balloon can do, read off one Ditko Spider-Man page (a reader's contribution).
  - The [lettering category](https://balloontales.com/category/tips/lettering/) lists the rest by balloon type: rough, sawtooth, jagged burst, dashed, "TV shape", overlapping and "trapped inside" balloons, tattered captions, bouncy text, multiple-outline and open sound effects.
- **[Better Letterer](https://blambot.com/pages/lettering-tips)** — Nate Piekos (Blambot). Twenty-four numbered infographics on one page, each one tip with right and wrong examples. Teaches the rules that mark professional lettering. The tips have no addresses of their own. *(also in sequential-art-learning-resources.md; the titles were read in this pass)*
  - Balloons and tails: #004 Don't Cross Balloon Tails, #006 Consistent Balloon Tails, #013 Layering Balloons in a Conversation, #015 Two Schools of Balloon Making: Layer vs. Stroke, #016 Consistency of Radio & Thought Balloons, #019 Symmetrical vs. Organic Balloons.
  - Text inside the balloon: #005 Properly Stacking Text in Dialogue Balloons (correct and incorrect stacks for sentences whose longest word comes first, in the middle or last), #007 Line and Negative Space in Comics Dialogue, #012 The 94% Line Width Cheat, #017 Visual Differences in Point Sizes.
  - Sound effects: #003 Dynamic Sound Effects (one "BAWHOOM" built in nine steps: type it, break the letters apart, find the source of the explosion, turn the letters along its lines of force, enlarge the vowels, add two offset outlines, trim against the border), #021 Nate's Color Palette for Sound Effects.
  - Grammar and punctuation: #001 I vs. I, #008 Non-English Dialogue in English Language Comics, #009 Proper Use of Quotation Marks in Comics, #010 Using Italics in Comic Dialogue, #011 Ellipses and Double Dashes in Comics Dialogue.
  - Type and production: #002 Kerning!, #014 Comic Lettering Tips: The Essentials, #018 Typographic Terminology, #020 Enabling Autoligatures & Contextual Alternates, #022 Traditional Comic Book Hand Lettering Pens and Stroke Directions, #023 Proper Comic Book Script Preparation for Letterers, #024 How to Combat "Press Gain" When Printing Small, Light-Colored Text on Dark-Colored Backgrounds.
- **[Lettering In Adobe Illustrator: Four](https://clintflickerlettering.blogspot.com/2010/10/lettering-in-adobe-illustrator-four.html)** — Jim Campbell (letterer, twice nominated for the Eisner by his blog's account). Step-by-step article, part four of six: basic balloons and captions. Teaches a balloon from an ellipse flattened by counted nudges, straight and curved tails with the pen tool, merging, cropping a balloon to the top or bottom border, and captions indented from the border; the tail points toward the mouth without having to reach it.
  - [Five](https://clintflickerlettering.blogspot.com/2010/11/lettering-in-adobe-illustrator-five.html): twelve non-standard balloons and how each is built: joined, linked, double outline, breakout, two radio balloons, burst, manga-style burst, thought, whisper, weak and robot.
  - [Six](https://clintflickerlettering.blogspot.com/2010/11/lettering-in-adobe-illustrator-six.html): sound effects. One default effects font per job plus a hard and a soft one; shaping, outline, inline, gradient, effects on a path, hollow effects, masking an effect behind the art; and why not to give a sound a soft drop shadow.
  - Parts [One](https://clintflickerlettering.blogspot.com/2010/10/lettering-in-adobe-illustrator-one.html), [Two](https://clintflickerlettering.blogspot.com/2010/10/lettering-in-adobe-illustrator-two.html) and [Three](https://clintflickerlettering.blogspot.com/2010/10/lettering-in-adobe-illustrator-three.html) cover script preparation, document set-up and setting type; they are listed on the blog and were not read.

## C. Visual Storytelling: Emanata, Framing and Mood Lighting

- **[Storytelling with The Odyssey of Sergeant Jack Brennan](https://sequentialartistsworkshop.thinkific.com/courses/comicsodyssey)** — Tom Hart (cartoonist, director of the Sequential Artists Workshop). Free course of 18 lessons. Its first unit, "How Pictures Work", teaches the composition of one panel in order: Balance and Symmetry in Picture Composition; Angles and diagonals. Danger and Drama; The Shot; Framing; Staging: Where to Put the Characters. The page offers it through a "Get Started for Free" enrolment, so it probably needs an account. *(also in sequential-art-learning-resources.md)*
- **[Emanata & !#@?$%!](https://salgoodsam.com/mc/emanata)** — Salgood Sam (cartoonist with Marvel, DC and Image credits; comics teacher at Syn Studio, by his site's bio). Article on his Making Comics teaching site. Teaches what emanata and grawlixes are and why they spare the drawing some acting, with sets of symbols from several sources; the exercise is to fill a sketchbook page or two with emanata of your own invention.
- **[Manga Morphology](https://www.visuallanguagelab.com/jvl)** — Neil Cohn (comics researcher, Visual Language Lab). Reference list. Teaches the manga counterpart of emanata: a catalogue of emotion symbols (anger veins, sweat drops, dread lines, blush, spiral eyes), each with its meaning and where on the body it attaches. *(also in sequential-art-learning-resources.md)*
- **[Colour Pop and Fade](https://comicsdevices.com/colour-pop-and-fade/)** — Reimena Yee (Comics Devices library). Reference entry with examples. Teaches colour as a control of focus: an object "pops" in colour at the moment a character notices it, and a group "fades" into one shared colour so that the pursued figure stands out, shown on *Cosmoknights*.
  - [Repoussoir](https://comicsdevices.com/repoussoir/): a foreground element at the left or right edge that leads the eye into the panel.
  - [Visual Morphemes or Emanata](https://comicsdevices.com/visual-morphemes-or-emanata/): the library's definition of emanata; the entry is short and points to manga symbols ("manpu") and to Cohn's list.
  - The [list of all devices](https://comicsdevices.com/whole/) has more for this list: Establishing Shot, Atmospheric Panel, Frame Panel, Large-Small Panels, Leitmotif.
- **[Basics of Comic Composition](https://www.proko.com/course-lesson/basics-of-comic-composition)** — David Finch (comics artist for Image, Marvel and DC). Free video lesson, 18:45, the first lesson of his paid course on Proko; a [12:02 cut](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5KX7WpRVtVU) is on Proko's YouTube channel. Teaches the rule of thirds and why it matters when a scene is drawn.
  - [Cinematic Storytelling and Compositional Pitfalls](https://www.proko.com/course-lesson/cinematic-storytelling-and-compositional-pitfalls) (free, 16:42; [7:51 cut](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b7BZNwI1DnI)): how the camera angle chosen for each panel shapes the story, and the common composition mistakes to avoid.
- **[Mastering Visual Storytelling - Angles, Shots, and Camerawork for Comics](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DO7g48W-GzI)** — Mike Hawthorne (comics artist, the cinematography instructor of Marvel's course on Proko). Video lesson, 14:03, on Proko's channel. Teaches shot choice as pacing: close-ups for emotional moments, long shots to establish a place, and worm's-eye and bird's-eye views to build atmosphere.
- **[Basic Staging Principles (part 1)](http://sevencamels.blogspot.com/2009/11/basic-staging-principles-part-1.html)** — Mark Kennedy (feature-animation story artist; the posts describe his work on Disney's *Home on the Range*). Blog article with film frames, written for storyboards and directly usable for panels. Teaches power through camera height and placement: an upshot makes a character large and menacing, a downshot small and weak, and the character higher in the frame reads as the stronger; he tells how moving a villain up in the frame fixed a scene.
  - [Basic Staging Principles (part 2)](http://sevencamels.blogspot.com/2009/11/basic-staging-principles-part-2.html): a wide shot with empty space for a lost or lonely character; flat staging reads as funny and staging in depth as dramatic; one drawing from *The Rescuers* restaged to show the face and the danger at once.
  - [Contrast and Composition](http://sevencamels.blogspot.com/2007/01/when-you-first-approach-drawing-think.html): building a picture on one contrast (big against small, detailed against plain) and steering the eye in a loop, read off one painting.
  - [Layout help from Carl Barks](https://sevencamels.blogspot.com/2006/03/layout-help-from-carl-barks.html): composing a layout in three levels (foreground, middle ground, background) and not around a finished figure.
- **[How to Draw CAR CHASES](https://www.clipstudio.net/how-to-draw/archives/160989)** — The Etherington Brothers (artists and authors of the *How to Think When You Draw* books; Art Rocket, Celsys). Illustrated article. Teaches camera choices for action in a single panel: the establishing shot, deep perspective, foreground framing, opposed tilts and cutting back to the driver. *(also in sequential-art-learning-resources.md)*
  - [How to Draw GRASS & FORESTS](https://www.clipstudio.net/how-to-draw/archives/167907): cast shadows that imply a canopy outside the panel, set the focus and separate foreground, middle ground and background.
- **[Strip Panel Naked: framing, blocking and focus](https://www.youtube.com/@StripPanelNaked/videos)** — Hassan Otsmane-Elhaou. Video essays on published pages. Together they teach where to put the reader's eye and the characters.
  - [Framing Characters in Human Target | Greg Smallwood](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SCICh_Hgmmg) (7:30): how characters are cropped, and a camera kept on the lead's eyeline. *(also in sequential-art-learning-resources.md)*
  - [Central Framing in Moon Knight](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m9FikCortQI) (7:58): pages of full-width panels with the subject placed centrally, and the alternative.
  - [Three Ways to Create Focus in Comics | Punisher](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=O7vKb2XBBv0) (6:51): a simulated depth-of-field blur, pure silhouettes, and feature panels that bleed through the page.
  - [Blocking a Scene in a Comic Book | Vision (2015)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VlzZBBivGQY) (7:36): positions of the characters that show a changing relationship.
  - [Using 3D Space in 2D Comic Books](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Bd0Pu0Syik4) (6:20): foreground, middle ground and background in nearly every panel of *We3*, and movement shown from back to front.
  - [Framing Locations | Deadly Class](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SVVwoCj5YUE) (5:47): one colour per location, so that backgrounds can be dropped without losing the place.
  - [The Power of Silhouettes and Lines in Comic Art](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8fYPPF4oX18) (7:25): line quality and readable silhouettes as carriers of mood.
  - [Creating Acting in Comics](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Fpsl5ogfbRI) (6:33): composition, juxtaposition and body acting in *Blacksad*.
- **[Basics of Comic Shading](https://www.proko.com/course-lesson/basics-of-comic-shading)** — David Finch. Free video lesson, lesson 5 of his paid course on Proko; a [7:31 cut](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=imHSlc_aYAw) is on Proko's YouTube channel. Teaches the steps of placing shadows that work and the difference between a cast shadow and a body shadow.
- **[STOP Guessing Your Shadows: How To Light A Scene](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ij4GI5gSLBc)** — David Finch. Video tutorial, 33:01, on his own channel. Teaches lighting a scene so that shadows follow from a chosen light. The description says nothing about the content, so this summary rests on the title alone.
- **[How to Use Lighting and Composition in Comics | The Walking Dead | Strip Panel Naked](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zND4iSx1P3E)** — Hassan Otsmane-Elhaou. Video essay, 10:44. Teaches hard against soft lighting and the switch between them for dramatic effect, on two passages of issue #85, and how two artists' styles call for different lighting choices.
  - [Representing Emotion with Colour | Black Monday Murders](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=myIcxizHSr4) (5:26): red and blue used across scenes to steer the reader's feeling.
  - [Colour as Subtext | Pretty Deadly (2013) - Jordie Bellaire](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ua92kh38wbM) (6:09): a limited palette, and colours that stand for themes.
  - [Colour From Another World | Paper Girls](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9FP2XpoqfVo) (7:13): how Matt Wilson builds the book's look on a palette of complementary colours; the description read here is cut off after that.
- **[A "Formula" for Coloring Light (multiple light sources)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8haj-UMfyxw)** — K. Michael Russell (comics colourist; channel "Color with Kurt"). Video tutorial, 19:26. Teaches a way to think through a scene with more than one light, with examples from five working colourists.
  - [How to Add Color & Light in Shadows](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TU4KEPKuc40) (14:55): a second light source treated as contained inside the shadows already set, and where its highlights go.
  - [Avoid 2D lighting a 3D world when coloring!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=efhbXEEhVYQ) (11:05): lighting the elements of a panel as forms in space and not as flat shapes.
  - [How To Color Comic Books! More Storytelling with Color](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aWI2bAKM6rQ) (19:44): colour choices that serve the story; the description is mostly links, so this rests on the title.
- **[How to Use Color Theory for Comics](https://proko.com/course-lesson/how-to-use-color-theory-for-comics)** — Matthew Wilson (comics colourist; Marvel's course on Proko). Free video lesson. Teaches using hue, saturation and value to fix a colour problem on a page, and monochromatic, complementary and triadic schemes. *(also in sequential-art-learning-resources.md)*
- **[Shapewelding](http://gurneyjourney.blogspot.com/2007/10/shape-welding.html)** — James Gurney (illustrator, author of *Dinotopia* and *Color and Light*). Blog article with paintings; it is about painting, not comics, and it applies directly to spotting blacks and to mood lighting. Teaches how to keep a crowded picture simple by letting neighbouring light shapes and dark shapes merge into a few large masses, with the strongest contrast kept for the centre of interest; shown on Howard Pyle and others.
  - [Counterchange](https://gurneyjourney.blogspot.com/2008/04/counterchange.html): a form that is light against a dark ground at one end and dark against a light ground at the other, on five pictures.
  - [Contre-Jour Lighting](http://gurneyjourney.blogspot.com/2007/10/contre-jour-lighting.html): backlight. The subject becomes a silhouette, its colours weaken, its shadows reach toward the viewer and detail is lost in the glare at the edges.
