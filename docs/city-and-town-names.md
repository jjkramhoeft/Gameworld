# City and town names

Fifty invented names of cities and towns for each of the 28 races of the default list,
1,400 in all. Each race has a short note on what its names are built from, drawn from its
entry in [races-basis.md](races-basis.md), and then the names.

This document is material, like [races-basis.md](races-basis.md) and
[tools-basis.md](tools-basis.md). It binds nothing and no generation rule reads it. The
city and town name generator ([society-generation.md](society-generation.md) section 5.3)
took the word parts and the patterns of each race from the notes below: its word lists
are `SettlementNameLexicons` in `src/Gameworld.Generation/Names`. The 50 names of a race
are not a list it draws from, so a generated name is seldom one of them.

- **Invented.** Every name is made up. The names of the human races borrow the sound and
  some word parts of the languages of the real people each is built on, as the races
  themselves do, but none is meant to be a real place. As in races-basis.md, no name has
  been searched for earlier use. Names close to well-known real or fictional places were
  left out where they were noticed.
- **Plain letters.** The names use the letters A to Z, spaces, hyphens and apostrophes,
  and nothing else, so that any of them can be used as it stands.
- **Cities and towns alike.** A name does not say whether a place is a city or a town.
  Some sound larger (a gate, a hold, a castra, -cheng, -pura) and some smaller (a hollow,
  -by, -bougou), and a rule may use that or ignore it.
- **No repeats.** No name appears twice, within a race or across races.
- **The new races.** Their names are mostly plain English compounds, like the names of
  the races themselves, and stand for the names the places carry in the trade tongue. The
  Hoarkin and the Twofold also have names in their own tongue.

## 4. Sunlanders (`sunlanders`)

Many tongues over a wide land, so the names vary more than those of any other human race.
The forest city-states favour long, vowel-rich names, some with ile- "house" or ilu-
"town". The grassland kingdoms use -dugu, -bougou and -koro, "village" and "old town" in
the savanna tongues, and -kro "town". The cattle country of the south and east uses Mb-,
Ng-, Nt- and Nk- and endings like -ombe and -ezi. Some names carry the iron, gold, cattle
and praise of the lore: Ile-Irinmo (irin, iron), Iluwura (wura, gold), Ngombezi (ngombe,
cattle), Djelikoro (djeli, praise-singer).

## 7. Canal folk (`canalFolk`)

## 8. Elves (`elf`)

## 9. Dwarves (`dwarf`)

Two kinds of name. The hold's own name, in the dwarven tongue, is short and hard, of
stopped consonants and rolled r, and the same few words recur: grund rock floor, dun hall,
rak and drak deep, gund and bund delved: Durmagrund, Thol Varrak, Haddrak, Varnagund. The
gate name, used by the surface peoples at the market town below the gate, is a plain
compound about the hold's craft, its records and its stores: Sevengrain Gate, Ledgerdeep,
Oathplate, Ironcistern, Silverbore, and Hungergate after the Hungry Gate.

## 10. Halflings (`halfling`)

## 11. Gnomes (`gnome`)

jokes: Great Escapement, Gnomon's Rest, Filigreen, Calipers Cross, Oddcog. A long name
such as Bobbinwick-under-Oak belongs to an old warren whose name has been added to for

## 13. Hoarkin (`hoarkin`)

and slow, of long vowels (aa, ee, ii, oo, uu) and the soft sounds h, l, m, n, s and v,

use at the cold table. They name the ice, the long sleep and the hunt: Twentybeats (the
resting heart), Long Lying, Seal Breath, Moonwake (they wake once each moon), First

## 17. Brinefolk (`brinefolk`)

The Brinefolk name their towns as if the sea were still there: harbours, shores, tides,
reefs and shell beds on a dry salt flat (Dryharbour, Ebbmark, Lowshore, Oldreef,
Tidepromise). The rest are of salt and the pan works: pan, cake, crust, brine, halite,
gypsum, soda glass, the stamped cakes they pay in, and Bitterns, the bitter liquor left
when the salt is drawn. Onestorey and Seated Barrows keep the law of the bed: build low,
bury the dead sitting.

## 18. Duskreaders (`duskreaders`)

Names of dusk, night, cool and heat, of canyons, cellars and the walls that are read at
nightfall: Duskwall, Heatscript, Fading Wall, Walltale, Coolseep, and Darkseep, since a
cool seep shows dark to their eyes. Their vow shows in Unspoken, Vowstone, Mute Hollow and
Silent Eye, and Blindnoon is the dazzle of full day. The Ashen Years, which they remember

## 22. Swiftborn (`swiftborn`)

generation: Ringtower, Long Work Hill, Handover, Torchpass, Fifthgen. Ashborn recalls the
generation of the Ashen Years, and the vines and olives the province they were made to

## 25. Stillfaces (`stillfaces`)

Their spoken tongue is a borrowed human one, used flatly, and their place names show it. A
name is a plain description (Low Village, Second Hollow, Southslope Village, Boundary
Posts) or what the place smells of, said as a phrase (Pine and Rain, Rain on Birch, Wet
Stone After Rain, Nettle and Clover). Still air, scent and the unmoving face give the
rest: Stillhollow, Windless, Unmasked, Facewood. Silence's End and Cindermemory recall the

## 26. Twofold (`twofold`)

Each Twofold is both, and their god has two faces, so their names in their own tongue read

Turninghouse, Goldthroat and Indigo Hall (the two colours of the throat patch),
Stayingfield, Goingroad, Chainhouse, Twofaces, Bothways.
