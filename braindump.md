## Research Graphic novels (Sequential art)

### Page Layout Components

* Panels. These are the individual boxes or frames that contain a single moment or scene in the story. Their size and shape help control the pacing and mood of the action.

* Gutters. These are the blank spaces between the panels. Readers use the gutters to mentally connect the action from one panel to the next, a process called closure.

* Splash Page. This is a full-page illustration used to introduce a major scene, setting, or dramatic moment. A double-page spread stretches a single image across two facing pages.

* Bleed. This happens when an image extends all the way to the very edge of the page without a border. It creates a sense of scale and removes boundaries from the scene.

### Text and Dialogue Components

* Speech Balloons. These rounded shapes contain the spoken words of the characters. A pointer or tail directs the balloon toward the character who is speaking.

* Thought Bubbles. These are cloud-like shapes with small circles leading to a character's head. They reveal a character's internal thoughts without them speaking out loud.

* Captions. These are rectangular boxes separated from the main action. They typically provide narration, background information, or changes in time and location.

* Sound Effects. Often called onomatopoeia, these are stylized words drawn directly into the artwork. Words like "BOOM" or "CRACK" represent noises and add energy to the scene.

### Visual Storytelling Elements

* Emanata. These are small symbols or lines drawn around a character to show emotion or movement. Examples include sweat drops for nervousness, motion lines for speed, or jagged lines for shock.

* Lettering. This refers to the style, size, and boldness of the text. Letterers change font styles to show volume, tone of voice, or different languages.

* Color and Shading. The color palette and use of light and shadow set the emotional tone of the book. High-contrast black-and-white art creates a very different mood than bright, vibrant colors.


## Graphic short stories 
Graphic short stories are brief, self-contained narratives told through sequential art, typically ranging from 1 to 30 pages. Like written short stories, they focus on a single mood, a tight plot, or a brief slice-of-life moment rather than an epic saga.

### Key Characteristics

* Compact Pacing. Creators have limited pages, so every panel must quickly establish the setting, character, and conflict.
* Self-Contained Arc. Unlike ongoing comic book issues that end on cliffhangers, a graphic short story delivers a complete beginning, middle, and ending in one sitting.
* Experimental Freedom. Because they require less time to produce than full-length graphic novels, artists often use short stories to test bold art styles, unusual page layouts, or unconventional storytelling.
